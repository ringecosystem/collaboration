### Experiment Notes

#### AI-Assisted RCG Content Publishing

JDubs is developing a workflow that uses Codex to help prepare and publish RingDAO Community Guild content to the RCG X account more efficiently. The current blocker is account ownership: JDubs does not own the RCG X account and therefore cannot directly establish all required integrations. The revised approach under consideration uses Buffer as the publishing service and IFTTT as the automation layer, avoiding the need for a paid X developer account. The workflow remains unshipped while the team works through permissions, integration design, human approval, and publishing reliability.

#### KtonDAO Community Strategy and Engagement

Saso's research identified community participation as KtonDAO's primary challenge. The analysis cited approximately 67,000 KTON staked across versions 1 and 2, approximately 31,438 KTON delegated to governance, about 14 active contributors, and typical proposal participation of three to six voters. Against that backdrop, the proposed unwinding of RingDAO's cooperation with KtonDAO would remove the annual RING staking incentive, stop new RING deposits for KTON issuance, and phase out Kton Staking functions. This would require KtonDAO to operate more independently while also creating an opportunity for the community to reconsider KTON's utility and tokenomics through governance.

Given the community's size, limited treasury, and lack of development leadership, the analysis recommends beginning with simple, low-cost activities:

1. Publish a monthly community report covering KtonDAO, the wider ecosystem, and treasury activity.
2. Create an onboarding guide explaining how to acquire KTON, delegate, vote, and submit proposals.
3. Reestablish a consistent X presence through weekly educational, historical, and governance-focused posts.
4. Explore a symbolic KTON burn event, small community-content grants, KTON history storytelling, and a short monthly voice roundtable.
5. Retain the proposed KTON Sentinel AI CFO concept as a future proof of concept, without committing treasury funds until development capacity is available.

Saso also evaluated a proposed engagement game requiring a 1 KTON entry fee. The analysis concluded that a paid entry would be unlikely to filter participation meaningfully, could further reduce participation in a small community, and could introduce lottery or gambling concerns where entry leads to a random prize. The proposed revision is a free-to-play model with an optional KTON burn for bonus participation. Possible formats include price predictions, governance participation rewards, treasure hunts, meme competitions, and screenshot challenges. The concept remains exploratory and requires governance, legal-risk, and implementation review before use.
