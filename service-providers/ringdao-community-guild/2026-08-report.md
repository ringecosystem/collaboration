### Workflow Notes

#### AI-Assisted X Publishing

- **Problem addressed:** Preparing and publishing the weekly RCG Quiz Game tweet takes approximately six to eight minutes, while direct X API access requires a more expensive developer account.
- **How it works:** An AI assistant helps prepare the approved post text and image. A dependency-free Python tool uploads the image to Cloudinary, then sends the approved text and public image URL to an IFTTT Webhook. An IFTTT Pro applet publishes the post to the authorized X account.
- **Human review:** JDubs reviews the post text and image and must explicitly approve a live publication.
- **Community ownership:** The code, setup guide, tests, and safety instructions are maintained in the public [ai-agent-x-post-workflow repository](https://github.com/2xJDubs/ai-agent-x-post-workflow).
- **Known limitations:** The workflow requires IFTTT Pro, Cloudinary configuration, and authorization to connect the intended X account. The time reduction is based on JDubs' observed estimate during August testing rather than instrumented measurement.

### Experiment Notes

#### KtonDAO and Evolution Land Research

Saso spent August researching KtonDAO, with particular attention to a GitHub discussion about reviving Evolution Land. His intended next step was to organize a Discord call to test community sentiment and determine whether enough interest existed to develop the concept further. He was prepared to hold an initial discussion even if only he and BallslikeKingKong attended, but recognized that the proposal would require participation from more than two active people before it could move beyond early exploration.

The preliminary sustainability concept was built around KTON and assumed that its price could remain near $1 during the initiative's early stage. After KTON fell by more than 70%, that premise was no longer viable. Saso therefore paused the Evolution Land revival concept rather than polishing or promoting a plan whose participation and economic foundations were not strong enough. In September, he plans to brainstorm new ideas and continue using `hast.agent` as a support tool where useful.

#### Laki's Task-Level AI Assistance

Laki continued his community management and engagement responsibilities throughout August. He used ChatGPT selectively rather than deploying an agent or reusable workflow. For some weekly Discord quizzes, he asked ChatGPT to generate three plausible incorrect answers so that the multiple-choice questions would be more challenging. He also used ChatGPT to correct complex or important translations when Google Translate appeared too literal. These uses supported content quality and saved an estimated 10% across approximately 30 minutes of related work each week, but were not formalized into a documented workflow during August.

#### Deleted-Message Discord Moderation Bot

JDubs continued building a narrow-purpose Discord bot with assistance from Codex and his self-hosted Hermes Agent. RingDAO community servers are configured so users cannot create threads, and MEE6 automatically deletes messages containing links. This helps stop scammers from directing community members to malicious servers or ticketing locations, but it also means that messages deleted by MEE6 or by the scammers themselves are difficult for administrators to review afterward. Without that evidence, identifying and banning the responsible accounts is harder.

MEE6 can provide deleted-message logging, but enabling the required capability would require a paid subscription for every server where it is used. JDubs is therefore developing a focused alternative that performs this single function. The bot remained unshipped in August and is expected to enter testing in September. Before production deployment, the team should verify that access is restricted to authorized administrators and establish appropriate privacy and message-retention safeguards.
